R407C uses authoritative bubble/dew saturation tables (Tega/Freon, R32/125/134a): superheat references the DEW point at suction pressure, subcooling the BUBBLE point at discharge pressure, correctly accounting for its temperature glide. Other refrigerants are computed via the Ambrose-Walton corresponding-states method, anchored to each fluid critical point and validated against published manufacturer PT charts (pure fluids within ~1%). The remaining blends (R404A, R410A, R454B, R513A, R452A) currently use a single pseudo-pure column; for precise superheat/subcooling on these, treat values as approximate pending bubble/dew tables. R744 (CO2) is undefined above its critical point (~31C). Always verify against certified property data such as NIST REFPROP for compliance work.

T inlet + T outlet

Immersion or insulated clamp-on PT100. Measure close to heat exchanger. Even 0.1 C error at 2 C dT = 5% Q error - 4 decimals matters here.

2

Flow (L/s) + glycol %

Ultrasonic clamp-on preferred. Use refractometer to verify actual glycol %. Wrong concentration = wrong cp = wrong Q. Calculator adjusts cp and density automatically.

Hot side (condenser water / glycol)
3

T inlet + T outlet

Same method as cold side. Q_hot is always larger than Q_cold by the compressor input power. If Q_hot is less than Q_cold something is wrong.

4

Flow (L/s) + glycol %

Verify glycol % on hot side separately - concentrations may differ between circuits. Use refractometer.

Energy balance: Q_hot = Q_cold + P_el. Deviation over 5-8%: check flow measurements first. Small dT with high flow is much more sensitive to errors than large dT with low flow. At dT = 2 C, a 0.1 C sensor error = 5% Q error.
